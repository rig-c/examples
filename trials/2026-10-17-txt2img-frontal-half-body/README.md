# 2026-10-17 txt2img-frontal-half-body: can a txt2img recipe draw a frontal half-body desk painting?

## Question

Does a txt2img recipe — one public SDXL-family checkpoint, a fixed prompt and
negative prompt, eight seeds, run once without and once with an OpenPose
ControlNet — produce a frontal half-body painting of a girl sitting at a desk
with her forearms on it that passes a frontal-pose bar of **shoulder ratio
≥ 0.95 and eye Δy ≤ 3 px**, and, as a second rule, **head ≥ 1/3 of the frame
height**?

The painting was wanted as the source art for a head-turn demo rig, which needs
a face that starts square to the viewer. No `@rig-c` package was run; the
question is whether the art can be generated to the bar the rig needs.

## Measurement

Bars, stated before measuring, by the request that ordered the paintings:
shoulder ratio ≥ 0.95, eye Δy ≤ 3 px, head ≥ 1/3 of the frame. The request named
the bars but not the formulas; `measure.py`'s docstring (repeated in
`measure.json` → `definitions`) fixes them, all in painting px (1664×2432):

- `shoulder_ratio` = min(dL, dR) / max(dL, dR), dX = |shoulder.x − nose.x| —
  symmetry about the face's midline. There is no standing reference figure to
  divide by, so this denominator is the measuring run's choice.
- `eye_dy` = |l_eye.y − r_eye.y| from the body keypoints — the bar's reading.
- `eye_dy_face` = the same Δy from the mean of each eye's six face-landmark
  points — a second reading added because of the grid below; it is not the bar.
- `head_frac` = (chin.y − first non-background row between the ear points) / H.
- `ctrl_err` (pose set) = distance from each control point × 2 to the measured joint.

## Verdict

**FAIL** — 3 of 16 paintings pass the frontal bar (shoulder ratio ≥ 0.95 and
eye Δy ≤ 3 px: free 3, free 8, pose 5) and 2 of 16 pass it with head ≥ 1/3 as
well (free 3, free 8); with the OpenPose control the figures are 1/8 and 0/8,
the one frontal pass (pose 5) having a head of 0.290. A recipe that hits its bar
on 2 seeds in 16 is not a recipe. The eye half of the bar was moreover set below
what the instrument can resolve: the detector places points on a 4.0–5.4 px
grid in these paintings, so the measured eye Δy was either 0.0 or at least
4.5 px, and "≤ 3 px" passed only when both eyes fell in the same cell.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Reusing the inherited negative prompt as it was.** It forbade this
  composition: `twin tails`, `upper body`, `sitting`, `crossed arms`,
  `grey background`, `arms touching body`, `close-up` and `portrait` are all in
  it, and the positive asks for long twintails, upper body, sitting, arms
  folded on a desk, a plain light grey background and a head of at least a
  third of the frame. Those 8 words were removed (`gen.py` `DROP`). This was
  decided by reading the two prompts against each other; no seed was run with
  the negative intact, so how much the 8 words would have cost is not measured.
- **Judging the eye line at "Δy ≤ 3 px" on the keypoint detector.** Rejected as
  a measurement the instrument cannot make: the detector's y step is 4.0–5.4 px
  over all 133 points in every painting (`grid_step.json`), and the bar's
  reading took only the values 0.0 or ≥ 4.5. A 3 px bar on that grid is a test
  of whether both eyes rounded into the same cell. The measuring run added a
  second reading — the mean of each eye's six face-landmark points — which
  passes 6/16 instead of 3/16; it is reported, not substituted for the bar,
  because the bar was fixed before the run.
- **Reading the ear points as landing on the eyes**, and therefore discarding
  `ear_ratio` and ear visibility as unmeasurable. The measuring run reported
  this from face crops; it is contradicted on re-measurement. `ear_check.py`
  draws the points on all 16 paintings and finds each ear point 0.40–0.52
  interocular distances from the same-side eye, at the edge of the face beside
  the ear (`ear_check.jpg`). So `ear_ratio` (0.877–1.000, `measure.json`) is a
  reading of the face-edge points and not a reading of the eyes. Whether the
  points sit on a visible ear or on hair covering it was not measured, so ear
  visibility remains unmeasured.
- **Drawing the control with `@rig-c/parts` `renderSkeleton`.** It requires all
  18 OpenPose points, and in this composition the hips and legs are behind the
  desk. `control.py` re-draws the same limb table, colours and stick width in
  PIL and skips the missing points; its output was not compared against
  `renderSkeleton`, because there is no 18-point input for the same pose.
- **Prompt iterations v1–v3 on the free set** (records in `iterations/`, the
  reason for each `ADD` line is a comment in `gen.py`): v1 seed 1 drew two
  stacked comic panels with a grey-haired girl in a blazer; v2 seed 2 drew
  light-blue hair, a grey sweater and hands on the cheeks; v3 seeds 1 and 3
  drew a second, red-haired girl and seed 2 bled red into the hair; v4 seeds 2
  and 4 crossed the arms over the chest. Each was answered by negative-prompt
  words; the v4 answer is the final prompt, and the final free run covers the
  same seeds 1–4 again under it.

## Limits

- 16 paintings: one checkpoint, one prompt, seeds 1–8, with and without one
  control image. Another checkpoint, sampler, cfg, ControlNet strength or end
  point was not tried.
- The prompt was tuned on free seeds 1–4, which are also four of the eight
  final free seeds; that biases the free set towards passing (free 1 still drew
  two people).
- The two-person, X-arm, ribbon-colour, desk, eyes-open and background
  observations are by eye on the sheet; only the person count is also measured.
- The shoulder-ratio formula was chosen by the measuring run, not by the bar's
  author; its sensitivity to the detector's x step (the same 4.0–5.4 px grid)
  is not measured.
- Head fraction uses the first non-background row between the face-edge points;
  it would misread a figure whose hair touches a background of the same
  colour. That did not occur by eye on these 16.
- The lesson this trial is kept for: **a bar set below the instrument's
  resolution does not measure the thing it names.** An eye-line bar has to be
  set at or above the detector's step on the image size it runs on (here
  4.0–5.4 px at 1664×2432), or it has to be read with an instrument fine enough
  to resolve it — and that choice belongs before the run, not after.
- Later passes on the same day that changed the hair wording asked a different
  question and are not part of this trial.
